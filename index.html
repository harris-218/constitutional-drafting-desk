Pre-Filled Cause & Effect Organizer: The Road to 1787

1. Structural Defect (Pre-filled Cause) 2. Historical Crisis / Immediate Effect 3. 1787 Constitutional Remedy
No Federal Taxation Power: Congress could only request voluntary requisitions from states. National debt soared; Continental Army veterans were left unpaid; credit collapsed abroad.
Absence of Independent Executive: Lacked a chief magistrate or permanent standing commander. National government was powerless to mobilize troops when Shays' Rebellion shut down courts.
Conflicting State Currencies & Tariffs: Each state minted paper money and taxed interstate goods. Interstate trade collapsed into hyperinflation and economic warfare among neighbor states.
Format 4: Advanced & AP Civics Extension

Comparative DBQ Inquiry: Anti-Federalist Brutus vs. Federalist No. 51

Document A: Anti-Federalist Brutus No. 1 (1787)

"In a republic of such vast extent as the United States, the people cannot be properly acquainted with the character of their rulers, nor will the rulers be in a situation to possess the confidence of the people... The immense powers of the legislature will swallow up the state assemblies and annihilate state sovereignty."

Document B: Federalist No. 51 (James Madison, 1788)

"If men were angels, no government would be necessary. In framing a government which is to be administered by men over men, the great difficulty lies in this: you must first enable the government to control the governed; and in the next place oblige it to control itself. Ambition must counteract ambition."

Prompt: Evaluate whether the Constitution of 1787 created a safeguarded republic as Madison argued, or an inevitable consolidation of dangerous power as feared by Brutus.
